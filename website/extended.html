Three-Wire Geometric Derivations

Delta Parallel Load Equivalent

For a three-wire delta load, the branch voltages are the measured line-to-line voltages. A parallel \(G\Gamma C\) branch model is

\[ i_{xy}=G_{xy}v_{xy}+\Gamma_{xy}\breve v_{xy}+C_{xy}\dot v_{xy}, \qquad xy\in\{ab,bc,ca\}, \]

with \(\Gamma=1/L\) and \(\breve v\) the time primitive. The measured terminal currents satisfy

\[ i_a=i_{ab}-i_{ca},\qquad i_b=i_{bc}-i_{ab},\qquad i_c=i_{ca}-i_{bc}. \]

For the analytical \(G\Gamma\) reference, choose \(v_{ab}\), \(v_{bc}\), \(i_a\), and \(i_b\) as independent measured coordinates and define the six-dimensional spacor

\[ \mathbf y = v_{ab}\mathbf e_1+v_{bc}\mathbf e_2+\breve v_{ab}\mathbf e_3+ \breve v_{bc}\mathbf e_4+i_a\mathbf e_5+i_b\mathbf e_6. \]

Since \(v_{ca}=-(v_{ab}+v_{bc})\), KCL gives

\[ \begin{aligned} i_a&=(G_{ab}+G_{ca})v_{ab}+G_{ca}v_{bc} +(\Gamma_{ab}+\Gamma_{ca})\breve v_{ab} +\Gamma_{ca}\breve v_{bc},\\ i_b&=-G_{ab}v_{ab}+G_{bc}v_{bc} -\Gamma_{ab}\breve v_{ab}+\Gamma_{bc}\breve v_{bc}. \end{aligned} \]

The trajectory lies in a four-dimensional subspace of the six-dimensional Euclidean measurement space. Let \(\mathbf K=\mathbf a\wedge\mathbf b\wedge\mathbf c\wedge\mathbf d\) be the model hypervolume and let \(\mathbf K_{\mathrm{osc}}\) be the osculating hypervolume computed from trajectory derivatives. If \(\mathbf K_{\mathrm{osc}}=\sum_I s_I\mathbf e_I\), the delta parallel parameters are obtained from

\[ \begin{aligned} G_{ab}&=\frac{s_{2346}}{s_{1234}}, & G_{bc}&=\frac{s_{1346}}{s_{1234}}, & G_{ca}&=\frac{s_{1345}}{s_{1234}},\\ \Gamma_{ab}&=\frac{s_{1246}}{s_{1234}}, & \Gamma_{bc}&=\frac{s_{1236}}{s_{1234}}, & \Gamma_{ca}&=\frac{s_{1235}}{s_{1234}} . \end{aligned} \]

Wye Series Load Equivalent

For a three-wire wye load without measured neutral, the directly resolvable family is the series form:

\[ v_{xn}=R_x i_x+L_x\dot i_x+S_x\breve i_x,\qquad x\in\{a,b,c\}. \]

The neutral voltage is eliminated with line-to-line voltage equations. For the \(RL\) analytical reference:

\[ \begin{aligned} v_{ab} &= R_a i_a+L_a\dot i_a-R_b i_b-L_b\dot i_b,\\ v_{bc} &= R_b i_b+L_b\dot i_b +R_c(i_a+i_b)+L_c(\dot i_a+\dot i_b). \end{aligned} \]

The corresponding spacor is

\[ \mathbf z = i_a\mathbf e_1+i_b\mathbf e_2+\dot i_a\mathbf e_3+ \dot i_b\mathbf e_4+v_{ab}\mathbf e_5+v_{bc}\mathbf e_6 . \]

Comparing the model hypervolume with the osculating hypervolume gives

\[ \begin{aligned} R_a&=\frac{s_{2345}}{s_{1234}}, & R_b&=\frac{s_{1345}}{s_{1234}}, & R_c&=\frac{s_{2346}}{s_{1234}},\\ L_a&=-\frac{s_{1245}}{s_{1234}}, & L_b&=\frac{s_{1235}}{s_{1234}}, & L_c&=\frac{s_{1246}}{s_{1234}} . \end{aligned} \]

Structural Singularities

A parallel wye family is structurally singular in a three-wire measurement because the required phase-to-neutral voltages are not unique. Adding an arbitrary common-mode function \(u(t)\) to all phase potentials preserves the measured line-to-line voltages but changes the branch voltages of a parallel wye model.

A series delta family is structurally singular because the internal branch currents are not uniquely observable. The transformation \((i_{ab},i_{bc},i_{ca})\mapsto(i_{ab}+h,i_{bc}+h,i_{ca}+h)\) preserves the measured terminal currents for any circulating component \(h(t)\), while a series branch law depends on the internal branch current itself.

Virtual-Neutral Interpretation

A virtual-neutral reference can be used when a line-to-line load is better interpreted through a wye series representation. The virtual node is not a measured conductor; it is a reference that allows branch sums in the wye representation to recover the original line-to-line values. This is the interpretation used by the virtual-neutral analytical example in the paper.

Windowed Matrix Implementation

The analytical quotients above document the geometric origin of the method. The sampled implementation evaluates the same KCL/KVL content over finite windows:

\[ \mathbf y_w=\Phi_w\theta+\epsilon_w . \]

Columns and outputs are scaled before the least-squares solve so that the reported condition number reflects information content rather than the arbitrary units of volts, amperes, henries, or farads. Derivative and primitive coordinates are obtained by window-local FIR differentiation, polynomial filtering, regularized finite differences, or harmonic projection followed by analytical differentiation.

Energy-Admissibility Criterion

A numerically fitted equivalent is accepted only if it closes the element-level energy balance over the same observation window:

\[ \rho_E = \frac{ \left\|p_{\mathrm{PCC}}(t)-p_{\mathrm d}(t)-dW(t)/dt\right\|_2 }{ \left\|p_{\mathrm{PCC}}(t)\right\|_2+\varepsilon_E }. \]

Small \(\rho_E\) means that terminal power can be accounted for as Joule dissipation plus the time derivative of stored electromagnetic energy in the identified elements. Large \(\rho_E\) rejects a candidate even if a least-squares solution exists.
